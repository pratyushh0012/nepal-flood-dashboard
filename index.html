How the Bhotekoshi flood affected children's food and schooling in Nepal

On 26 August 2026, floods hit the Bhotekoshi–Trishuli corridor in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts. This dashboard looks at what the flood meant for children's nutrition and education, and what it could mean for Nepal's progress on SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and SDG 4 (Quality Education).

Child stunting in Nepal, 2001–2022

Stunting among children under 5 in Nepal, % (Nepal Demographic and Health Survey)

Stunting fell from 57% to 25% between 2001 and 2022. A disaster like this flood puts that progress at risk, because poor nutrition in early childhood can cause lasting stunting.

Conclusion

Disaster reports usually focus on deaths and damaged homes. The data here shows a longer-term cost. Families who lost crops and income are relying on relief food, more children are malnourished, and damaged schools mean children are missing both lessons and school meals. Recovery plans should address nutrition and education together, alongside clean water (SDG 6) and flood resilience (SDG 13), because the flood affected all of them at once.

A flood is usually counted in deaths and destroyed homes. The longer cost is quieter. Families who lose fields and income feed children on relief noodles, malnutrition rises, and with schools washed away the school meal and the classroom disappear together. Recovery that rebuilds schools without restoring nutrition, or feeds children without getting them back to class, will fix only half the problem. In Nepal's flood zones, Zero Hunger and Quality Education have to be rebuilt together.
